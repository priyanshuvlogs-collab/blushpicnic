You've chosen the date and the person. Now you're looking at a park on a map and wondering whether you're allowed to set up a low table, flowers and cushions there. The honest answer is *it depends*, and the useful part is knowing what it depends on. This guide sticks to what the City of Toronto itself says on its park rules page, plus our own booking policies. It is not legal advice, and the City can change its rules, so confirm anything important with Toronto Parks, Forestry and Recreation before the day.

## The one number to remember: 25 people

The City's park rules say a permit is required for "large-scale gatherings, events or picnics over 25 people". That is the line between an ordinary park visit and a permitted event. Two things follow from it:

- **A small, styled picnic for two to a handful of guests** is the sort of thing the rule isn't aimed at, but the rule isn't the only one that applies (see the next sections).
- **A bigger celebration** such as a family event, a shower or a corporate gathering can cross the line quickly. If your guest list is anywhere near 25, ask before you book rather than after.

The City also notes that permit groups take precedence over casual recreational use, and that picnic areas and fire pits can be booked. If you want a particular picnic area, booking it is a different thing from just turning up.

## Setting up: what counts as a "setup"?

Many guides skip this part. The City's page speaks plainly about tents, furniture and camping, and a styled picnic touches all three:

- **Tents and canopies.** The City says tents take up a lot of space in parks and on beaches, and suggests an umbrella or tree shade instead. If your design needs a canopy or a larger structure, don't assume it's fine. Ask first.
- **Park furniture.** The City says to leave park furniture such as picnic tables and chairs where they are. A low picnic table you bring yourself is a different thing from moving a park bench, so don't drag the park's furniture into your photo.
- **Camping.** Overnight camping is not allowed. A picnic is a day-time visit.
- **Noise.** There is no set volume limit on the page, but it asks you to keep music from portable speakers to a reasonable volume.

## Alcohol: the rule people miss

This is the one that surprises couples planning a proposal or an anniversary. The City says drinking and serving alcohol is not permitted in parks or on beaches except in designated areas, and permitted use needs a permit and approval from the Alcohol and Gaming Commission of Ontario. Separately, the City runs a pilot program that lets people aged 19 and older bring and drink their own alcohol at some City parks.

So "a bottle of bubbly for the toast" isn't automatically fine and isn't automatically banned. It depends on the park and on the pilot list at the time you go. Check the current list for the exact park, and keep to the City's wording if you're unsure.

## Fire, fireworks and other things to leave at home

- Personal fireworks are not allowed in parks or on beaches.
- Fire pits can be booked and have their own rules: keep the fire small, never leave it unattended and pour water over the whole fire before you leave.
- Portable propane barbecues are permitted at picnic areas, except on Toronto Island.

Candles are common in a romantic setup. The City's page doesn't mention them, so we can't tell you they are allowed everywhere. Ask about the specific park if candles matter to your plan, and keep them contained.

## What "styled" picnics change, and what they don't

A styled setup looks like an event but, for a small group, is usually closer to a very nice picnic. The things that make it more than a picnic are the ones to check:

1. **Group size.** Over 25 people means a permit.
2. **Structures.** Canopies, arches or tents that need space or staking.
3. **Alcohol.** Serving it in a park is regulated.
4. **Amplified sound.** Keep it to a reasonable level.
5. **Leaving no trace.** Everything you bring has to leave with you.

## A simple five-step check before you book

1. **Count the guests.** Is it 25 or fewer?
2. **List what you're bringing:** table, cushions, flowers, any canopy, any speaker, any alcohol.
3. **Pick the park** and read its page on the City's website for anything specific, such as booking a picnic area.
4. **Contact the City** (311 or Toronto Parks, Forestry and Recreation) if you have a canopy, a larger group or alcohol in the plan.
5. **Tell us** the park and date in the booking form so we can plan the setup around the rules.

## Why this matters on the day

A permit problem rarely shows up when you book. It shows up on the day, when someone from the park asks you to take something down.
